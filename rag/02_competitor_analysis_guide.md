# Competitor analysis guide

## Purpose
Competitor analysis shows how the market is already served, what customers expect, where products are weak, and how a new idea could differ.

## Types of competitors
- **Direct:** same customer, same problem, similar solution.
- **Indirect:** same problem, different solution (for example a spreadsheet instead of dedicated software).
- **Substitutes and the status quo:** doing nothing, hiring a person, or using free tools.
- **Potential entrants:** larger companies that could add the feature.

## What to collect for each competitor
1. Name, website, and short description.
2. Target customers and segments.
3. Main features and what is missing.
4. Pricing model and price points (free, subscription, commission, per-use).
5. Distribution (how customers find them).
6. Strengths and weaknesses, ideally from customer reviews and public statements.
7. Evidence of traction, such as funding news, user numbers, or hiring.
Always record the source URL and the date, because information changes quickly.

## Comparison table
Build a table with competitors as rows and features, price, target segment, and weaknesses as columns. Gaps in the table point to opportunities.

## Finding differentiation
A differentiator should be meaningful to a specific customer segment and hard to copy. Common routes: serve a niche segment better, reduce price through a different cost structure, offer a simpler or faster experience, use unique data, or integrate into an existing workflow.

## Warning signs
- Many strong competitors and no clear segment to focus on.
- Competitors with large budgets who could copy the feature quickly.
- A differentiator that customers do not care about.

## Output for evaluation
Summarise the competitive intensity (low, medium, high), list the top three competitors with sources, state the proposed differentiator, and note assumptions and confidence.
