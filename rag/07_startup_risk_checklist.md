# Startup and software project risk checklist

Use this list to find risks, rate each by likelihood and impact, and plan a response (avoid, reduce, transfer, or accept).

## Market risks
- Customers do not have the problem strongly enough to pay.
- The target segment is too small or hard to reach.
- Competitors respond quickly or already serve the segment well.
Mitigation: customer interviews, small paid pilots, a narrow first segment.

## Product risks
- The solution does not solve the problem better than alternatives.
- Users do not return after the first use.
Mitigation: test an MVP early, measure repeat use, iterate quickly.

## Technical risks
- The core feature is harder than expected (for example accuracy of an AI model).
- Dependence on one external API, with limits, price changes, or outages.
- Scalability, security, or data quality problems.
Mitigation: prototype the hardest part first, wrap external services behind interfaces, add caching and fallbacks.

## Data and AI risks
- Model errors or invented information (hallucination) presented as fact.
- Biased or outdated data.
- Privacy and misuse of personal data.
Mitigation: ground answers in cited sources, show confidence, keep humans in the loop, minimise personal data.

## Legal and regulatory risks
- Data-protection rules (for example GDPR and local laws).
- Licensing, intellectual property, or industry regulation (finance, health, education).
- Terms of service of platforms and APIs.
Mitigation: check rules early, record data sources and licences, seek professional advice for high-stakes cases.

## Financial risks
- Costs higher than expected; revenue slower than planned.
- Low margins from payment fees, cloud costs, or support.
Mitigation: simple cost model, usage limits, price testing.

## Team and execution risks
- Missing skills, unclear roles, unrealistic schedule, scope growth.
Mitigation: phased plan, clear ownership, a "do not build yet" list.

## Devil's advocate questions
What would have to be true for this to succeed? Which assumption, if wrong, kills the idea? Why have others failed here? What would a sceptical customer say?
