# Business model patterns and unit economics

## Common revenue models
- **Subscription (SaaS):** customers pay a recurring fee. Predictable revenue; success depends on retention.
- **Marketplace (commission):** the platform connects buyers and sellers and takes a percentage or fee per transaction. Needs both sides to join, which is the "cold start" problem.
- **Freemium:** a free tier attracts users and a paid tier earns revenue. Works when a small share of users converts and free users cost little to serve.
- **Pay-per-use:** customers pay for what they consume.
- **Advertising:** revenue from attention. Needs large audiences.
- **Licensing and enterprise sales:** higher prices and longer sales cycles.
- **Hardware plus services:** device sales combined with recurring services.

## Key terms
- **CAC (customer acquisition cost):** total sales and marketing cost divided by the number of new customers.
- **LTV (customer lifetime value):** expected gross profit from a customer over their relationship with the business.
- **Churn:** the share of customers (or revenue) lost in a period.
- **Gross margin:** (revenue minus direct cost of delivering the service) divided by revenue.
- **ARPU / ARPA:** average revenue per user or account.
- **Payback period:** time needed to earn back CAC from gross profit.
A commonly used rule of thumb is that LTV should be well above CAC, but the right ratio depends on the business and stage.

## Two-sided marketplaces
Strategies for the cold start: begin in one narrow location or category, supply the scarcer side first, offer a manual or concierge service at the start, and focus on repeat transactions to avoid users leaving the platform to transact directly.

## Pricing approaches
Cost-plus (cost plus margin), value-based (price linked to the value customers receive), competitor-based (relative to alternatives), and tiered pricing (different plans for different segments). Test prices with real customers.

## Business Model Canvas elements
Customer segments, value proposition, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partners, and cost structure. Use it to check that every part of the model fits together.

## What to check in an evaluation
Who pays, why, how much, and how often? What are the main costs? Is there a realistic path to a positive margin? Which assumptions have not been tested?
